Cassian was awoken by the thunderous clap. Heavy droplets of rain drummed continuously against the ship cabin's woven roof. 

*Storm again?* 

His stomach lurched with panic. "Is everybody safe?" he shouted out at Silvan outside, but the rain swallowed his words whole. 

Cassian swung his legs over the side and pushed himself up. Then he remembered.

The others were dead. All ten of them, both young and old, had died over the months they'd been at sea. Some had succumbed to sickness, others to the storm. Now, only the two brothers were left on the rickety, double-hulled catamaran. Two teenagers braving the open ocean.
<!--more-->

A viscous, hollow feeling replaced the panic in Cassian's gut. 

It was not anger - even though he understood that the village had sent them out into the open ocean to die, so that they would have enough to survive the winter. The brothers were orphans, expendable, and of little value to the village otherwise. The elders scorned them as delinquents. Dorel the blacksmith, broad-shouldered and soft-hearted, would excuse their misadventures as the restlessness of youth. And it was quite true. They never went looking for trouble. It just seemed to find them every time they put a toe out of line.

It was not fear either - with Silvan manning the sails and the worst of the oceanic storms having passed them, Cassian had little reason to be afraid. His brother might have been overly cautious at times, but he was nothing if not dependable. Without Silvan at his side, Cassian would have died a half-dozen times over - once to the sea, twice to the beasts, and thrice to their furious fellow villagers.

It was also not despair - just this morning they had spotted a flock of coastal birds on the horizon. They were too small and restless to be the huge, gliding predators of the open ocean. This could only mean that land - an island, a Basin, anything - was close. If the winds held, they should make landfall within days.

It was definitely not sadness - he'd barely known any of these people. Sure, Maren was kind enough to give the young ones extra servings from her bowl. And old lady Relia would call him *boy* without contempt. And Lucean sacrificed himself so that the last four would survive longer: Cassian, Silvan, and the twins. 
The twins were no more than five years old. Orphans, just like the brothers, unloved and uncared for all their short lives. *Did they feel loved during their final days?* Cassian wondered. He couldn't - wouldn't - think of their names.

Outside, the rain was still pattering on the sails. Cassian lay back down on the narrow, rope-strung cot. Silvan would need to sleep eventually, and when he did, Cassian would be the only one there to keep watch. He would need all the rest he could get. The water below was not as empty as they had once thought.
