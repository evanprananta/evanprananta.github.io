I can't say that writing had been a lifelong passion of mine. Throughout most of my life, I only wrote what I needed to: school assignments, notes, emails, presentation drafts. I only discovered the joy and utility of writing during the last periods of college.

Now, I write almost daily. Most of them are just nonsensical journaling. It's my way of figuring out what's going on in the day, what I've done, what to do next, etc. But periodically, I'd hit a rough patch of existential crisis or hard decisions. Every time, writing had helped me figure things out faster.

This habit had been so useful, so many times for me. Here's how.

## I write to collect knowledge.

I like to read. And every once in a while, I get to learn something useful or interesting. I'd then get this fear of letting go. "This new thing seems so interesting and meaningful. What if I forget it?" Some people get over this fear by taking tons of photos, collecting physical things, and hoarding books. I do it through writing.

Writing is a safe home for my thoughts and ideas to live in. Even though I rarely reread my old writings, I've always felt safe storing ideas and feelings in them.

Human beings are not great at keeping memories. Every time we recall a memory, some parts got erased, and some intensified. Written words don't have that problem. It doesn't matter whether it's a boring journal entry or a breakthrough idea. Writing things down preserve them.

*Actually, to consistently recall them, writing them down is not enough. But it is the first step.*

## I write to expand ideas.

While studying architecture in university, I needed to make a presentation quite often. I'd write my talking points down in preparation, but I'd always get more than what I had in mind. Ever since then, every time I got stuck at something, I'd write down my thoughts.

Writing makes it much easier to come up with new ideas and realize logical connections. It's almost like chemistry, where I put thoughts down on paper and see how they react together.

I believe there are two reasons for this. First, writing helps us structure our thoughts better. The mind has a way to *feel* busy without actually doing much work. Writing helps remove this illusion of the busy mind. What feels messy and complicated might be easily organized when we put it out of our heads.

Second, by writing the thoughts down, we have more space in our brain to think about other things. New ideas rarely come from linear thinking. But to allow parallel processing, we have to give our brain some space. Writing things down is a great way to let go of persistent thoughts.

## I write to express emotions.

I rarely write for emotional release. It's partly because writing about emotions *with* emotions has a way to amplify certain things. It makes me less objective.

That being said, writing had served me well in the past. It had helped me organize and express feelings that I didn't know how to process otherwise. Formulating feelings on a piece of writing is truly cathartic. The stronger the feelings, the more pleasurable the process, and the better the outcome.

## I write to share revelations.

Most of the things I write about are things that I've been wondering myself, for a long time: problems I faced, dilemmas, doubts and questions. Coming up with the answer is usually a hard and complicated process. That's why it's so satisfying when I'm finally able to explain the answer in a simple, succinct way through writing. Even though I could get there without sharing the work, I often find myself thinking, "I wish I'd known this earlier."

Changing people's minds is not part of why I write. I couldn't care less what strangers think. But it feels good to put something out there that I know would've been valuable to me. And if it's valuable to myself from the past, surely there's at least one person who would find it useful now.

## I write to better understand myself and the world.

> I write because I don’t know what I think until I read what I say.
> 
> – Flannery O’Connor

This is my most important reason for writing.

Writing helps me understand what I think and feel. Most of us *think* that we're thinking clearly. Most of the time, we're just blabbering our messy brains out. If only we'd put it down in writing, we will see how chaotic our thoughts can be.

Even though everybody's different, everybody should try writing things down when they don't know what to do. Getting things out of our heads is the first step to thinking clearly.

Writing for clarity is especially useful during hard times. Negative thoughts and emotions are incredibly persistent. They can quickly form a loop that makes us feel like we've got so much to worry about. In reality, what's in our brain is often just two bad things shouting after each other. But we'd go through one terrible scenario after another in our mind without ever seeing the big picture.

In these times, writing functions as an outlet for negativity and also as a mirror to our own irrationality. Very often, as we write things down, we can see things for what they are. When we can actually see the problems in their entirety, it usually makes them much less scary. We'll be able to zoom out of the trivial thoughts and see how clear the answer had been all along.

---

Ultimately, I write to help myself, to answer my own questions, to dispel my own insecurities, to process my own emotions, and clarify my own thoughts. And then I share it in the hope that it helps someone else.
