--- Sun-God, Mother of Gaius the world, Bringer of Light, Seeder of Life. ---

God looked at Her favorite son, Gaius. Now a beautiful blue marble, he'd calmed down after a seemingly never-ending period of rifts and explosions.  
Teenagers, God reminisced.  
She was happy that Gaius had calmed down. But after a billion rotations, he's getting boring to look at. He needs some rings to complement his beautiful blue oceans, God decided.
<!--more-->

So God sent out some of Her angels - Her ten favorite ones - to reshape Gaius without destroying him. The angels altered their orbits slightly, and after several hundred rotations, they struck Gaius one after another. Their impacts created huge craters which quickly filled with water from the surrounding oceans.

Over millions of rotations, these craters sank, crumbled, and stabilized into a series of Basins containing tall, mountainous rims, inland seas and lakes, and narrow straits connecting the inland seas to the open ocean.

As God had hoped, life flourished around and within these rich, nutrient-excreting Basins. More life evolved more quickly than ever before on Gaius. In time, intelligent creatures evolved both in water and on land. 

These smart little things are God's favorite. Although small, they're surprisingly unpredictable, a great source of fun in the otherwise cold and silent void of space.

Multiple intelligent species evolved, but two rose above the rest and killed the rest. One on land and one on water. Their differing habitats the only reason that they haven't killed each other off as they did their counterparts.

They spread across the Basins until no shore was empty of the water-dwellers and no rim was devoid of the land-dwellers. They developed different languages over time, but God thought of them using their original names - Rimari for the land-dwellers, and Nerevari for the water-dwellers.

Many times they tried to erase one another, as they had all others. But the Basins bound them together while their bodies kept them apart, so that neither could destroy the other without killing themselves.

So they remained. Not necessarily in peace, but in balance.

And God saw that it was good.
